About

I am a PhD candidate in geophysics at Stanford University. In my research, I study (1) radar techniques we can use to better understand planetary bodies, and (2) the planetary bodies themselves using existing radar datasets.

Before pursuing my PhD, I graduated from the University of Virginia, where I majored in environmental sciences and computer science and minored in astronomy.

Backstory

  1. Virginia

    Growing up

    As a kid, I was obsessed with astronomy. Some people say they "grew out" of a space phase. I never did (and never will).

  2. 2021 · University of Virginia

    I learn about remote sensing

    During my first year of undergrad, I learn about the field of remote sensing. I ask a remote sensing group on campus if they could use any help on research projects. I create interactive air pollution injustice maps to make satellite observations of air pollution more accessible.

  3. 2022-2023 · NASA JPL

    I discover my passion for planetary remote sensing

    I intern at NASA's Jet Propulsion Lab (JPL) creating a new data archive for the MARSIS mission. While working with the data, I discover my passion for planetary remote sensing.

  4. 2022-2024 · University of Virginia

    Exploring through class projects

    Through class projects from 2022 up until graduating from UVA, I use planetary mission datasets to pursue my own curiosities about Mars and Europa.

  5. 2023 · NASA JPL

    Back at JPL

    I intern in JPL's Exoplanet Discovery Group algorithmically assessing the quality and scientific utility of exoplanet transmission spectra, which broadens my view of both remote sensing and planetary science.

  6. 2024–present · Stanford University

    Where I am now

    I am now pursuing my PhD in Geophysics at Stanford, using radar remote sensing to investigate questions in planetary science.

Research

Venus

VERITAS

In my primary research project as part of Stanford's Radar Interferometry Group, I study how we can detect active surface deformation on Venus using Interferometric Synthetic Aperture Radar (InSAR) on NASA's upcoming VERITAS mission.

Europa · Jupiter's moon

Europa Clipper

In my secondary project with Stanford's Radio Glaciology Lab, I am studying strategies for REASON, Europa Clipper's radar instrument, to observe certain geologically interesting interfaces in Europa's ice shell.
